KASSIGNER SECURITY FIX RECORD 010

Finding:
Secret-memory cleanup and panic wiping were incomplete or inactive

Severity:
Medium

Branch:
fix/secret-memory-cleanup

Affected files:
bootloader/Cargo.toml
bootloader/Cargo.lock
bootloader/src/app/data.rs
bootloader/src/handlers/menu.rs
bootloader/src/handlers/tx.rs
bootloader/src/hw/lockdown.rs
bootloader/src/main.rs
bootloader/src/ui/seed_manager.rs
bootloader/src/ui/setup_wizard.rs

Problem before:
Cancelling dice collection set only count=0 and left every recorded roll in
RAM. Vec::clear released secret lengths without overwriting commit/reveal data.
Temporary entropy arrays were not consistently erased. The advertised panic
wipe was never called because esp-backtrace supplied the active panic handler.
That unused wipe also targeted a guessed broad SRAM range and could overwrite
the stack executing the wipe.

Fix after:
DiceCollector and PassphraseInput now provide volatile cleanup and invoke it on
drop. Dice cancel and rejection paths erase all roll storage. Temporary entropy
arrays are erased after mnemonic conversion. Commit/reveal buffers are
overwritten before their lengths are cleared.

AppData now has a targeted zeroize_sensitive method covering seed slots,
mnemonic indices, dice rolls, passphrases, private/account keys, raw-key input,
xprv output, transactions, signed QR data, steganographic passphrases,
encrypted/recovered backup buffers, message signatures, and commit/reveal data.
The heap-resident AppData pointer is registered once and the custom panic
handler invokes this exact cleanup before halting. Panic details are not sent
over USB.

Removed unsafe behavior:
The unused broad-address SRAM wipe and its unsupported anti-glitch claim were
removed. Cleanup no longer guesses where secrets or the active stack live.

Tests and verification:
New target self-tests confirm that dice zeroization removes the counter and all
roll values, and passphrase reset removes the length, cursor, page, and every
buffer byte. Cargo checks passed for Waveshare and M5Stack in normal,
verbose-boot, and production configurations.

Limit:
Panic cleanup is best effort. Instant power loss or severe memory corruption
can prevent code from running, while ordinary power loss removes volatile RAM
by hardware behavior.
